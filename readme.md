# UX Thinking: Trade Gold 24/7 and Oil 24/7

## The problem

Most traders think Gold and Oil close on weekends. The page has to change that idea quickly, then answer what a careful trader wants to know before investing:

1. What's new?
2. Why does it matter to me?
3. Can I trust this?
4. How do I start?

The page follows that order. Each section answers one question and leads to a "Start Trading" button.

## Page structure

- **Hero:** the offer and proof that the market is open right now.
- **Weekly timeline:** a simple visual comparing normal trading hours with 24/7.
- **Gold & Oil cards:** what you can trade.
- **Benefits:** what you gain.
- **Platforms:** the tools you already know.
- **Fast Start:** what happens after you sign up, in 3 steps.
- **Why TradeQuo:** licences and protection of your money.
- **FAQ:** honest answers about weekend prices and risks.
- **Final call to action.**

## Showing, not just saying

At the top, a small label reads "Gold & Oil are open · Sat 14:32" in the visitor's own time. Someone who visits on a Saturday sees the offer is true. Only this "open now" element moves on the page. The prices and chart are static and marked as examples, so nobody mistakes them for live data.

## Buttons

- **"Start Trading"** is the main action. It is the only red button.
- **"Try Demo"** is the second option for people who aren't ready yet. TradeQuo's own site already uses this pattern.
- **Smaller text links** handle the rest ("Log in", "Practise on demo first").

The main button appears at every key moment. On mobile, it stays fixed at the bottom of the screen once you scroll past the top section.

## What happens after "Start Trading"

Sign-up opens on the same page, so the user doesn't lose context.

- **Clear steps:** Sign up → Verify → Fund & trade. About 1 minute each.
- **The chosen market is remembered.** Clicking "Trade Gold" opens sign-up with Gold already selected.
- **The button and the risk warning stay visible** while the user fills in the form.
- **Existing clients have a quick log-in**, separate from sign-up.
- **It ends with a clear next step** ("Fund your account"), never a dead end.

## Talking about risk honestly

This is a financial product, so the page never promises profits. "Trading involves risk" appears close to every main button and in full in the footer. The FAQ explains openly that weekend prices can cost more and can jump on Monday. Licences and fund protection appear just before the final button, when people hesitate most.

## Brand and mobile

The page uses TradeQuo's colours, font, rounded buttons and cards. On mobile, the layout is stacked into one column, buttons are full width, and sign-up opens as a panel from the bottom of the screen.

# Assumptions

These are the things I assumed while designing. I would confirm each one with the TradeQuo team before going live.

## Who visits the page

1. **Two groups of visitors:** new people coming from the campaign, and existing TradeQuo clients. That's why the page has a clear sign-up and a separate, quick log-in.
2. **Most people visit on their phone,** coming from ads, social media or email. So I designed for mobile first.
3. **Visitors know a little about trading but aren't experts.** I used simple language and explained the risks in the FAQ.

## The product

4. **"24/7" includes Saturday and Sunday,** with possible short maintenance breaks.
5. **Gold means XAU/USD and Oil means WTI crude,** both traded as CFDs.
6. **Weekend trading works with a normal account.** No extra sign-up or fee.
7. **A free demo account exists,** and it includes weekend trading.
8. **Prices, trade sizes and the 4.9 rating on the page are examples,** and are labelled as such.

## Rules and regulation

9. **The risk warning (72.6% of accounts lose money) is current.** Compliance would need to approve the final text.
10. **The licence that applies depends on the visitor's country.** The licences shown come from tradequo.com.
11. **Verification needs a photo ID and proof of address before the first deposit.** The "about 3 minutes" comes from TradeQuo's existing Fast Start.

## Brand

12. **Colours, font, buttons and cards follow tradequo.com.**
13. **This page continues the existing "24/7 Gold" campaign** and adds Oil. It is not a new visual style.
14. **Gold and oil colours appear only in the illustrations,** never in buttons or text.

# What I Would Improve or Test

## How I'd know it works

The main goal is not clicks. It is **people who open an account and make a first deposit**.
I would also watch:

- Where users drop off during sign-up.
- How many new clients place a trade on a weekend.
- Complaints about weekend prices. If these go up, the page is overselling.

## What I would test

1. **Sign-up on the page vs. a separate page.** Which one leads to more deposits?
2. **The demo option.** Does offering "Practise on demo first" keep hesitant users, or does it delay real accounts?
3. **The sticky "Start Trading" button on mobile.** Does it help, or does it get in the way?
4. **Timing.** Do ads shown on Thursday and Friday, just before the weekend, work better?

## How I'd learn from users

- Show the hero for 5 seconds, then ask: what is this offer?
- Watch 5–6 traders use the page. After reading it, can they explain what "24/7" means and why weekend prices can differ?
- Check heatmaps and recordings to see where people stop scrolling or get stuck in the form.

## What I would improve next

1. **Real prices:** live prices clearly marked as live, plus a simple table of weekend trading hours and typical costs.
2. **More languages:** the page translated for TradeQuo's main markets.
3. **An option for visitors who aren't ready:** for example, a weekend price alert for Gold.
4. **Easier sign-up:** country filled in automatically, and the option to finish verification later on another device.
